Statutory Framework & Back-Billing Limits

Legally enforceable lookback barriers across supported jurisdictions

United Kingdom 12 Months
Ofgem Supply Licence SLC 21BA

Energy suppliers cannot charge or demand payment for unbilled gas or electricity consumed more than 12 months prior to the bill date. Exceptions apply if the consumer was obstructive or manifestly unreasonable, or if the contract was under 12 months.

Reference: Ofgem Energy Back-billing project • Binding Ombudsman dispute resolution after 8 weeks.
Australia (NERR Rule 30, 9 Mos) 9 Months
National Energy Retail Rules (NERR) Rule 30

Retailers in NERR participating states (NSW, QLD, SA) may only recover undercharged amounts for up to 9 months before the date the customer was notified of the undercharge. Note: Victoria has its own Energy Retail Code of Practice and is not covered. An exception applies if the customer was at fault.

Enforced by State Energy Ombudsmen (EWON, EWOQ, EWOSA).
United States - California (PG&E Rule 17.1, 3 Mos) 3 Months
PG&E Electric Rule 17.1 (SCE/SDG&E equivalent)

Investor-owned utilities are barred from billing residential accounts for more than 3 months of unbilled service resulting from utility billing delays or meter inaccuracies. Applies to residential undercharges due to billing error only.

Enforced by CPUC Consumer Affairs Branch.
United States - Texas (PUCT §25.28(d), 6 Mos) 6 Months
PUCT §25.28(d) (electric utilities); §25.480(e) for REPs

For electric utilities under PUCT §25.28(d), underbilling adjustments are limited to 6 months counted from the date the error was discovered. For retail electric providers, the limit under §25.480(e) must be verified with the PUCT.

Enforced by Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT).
